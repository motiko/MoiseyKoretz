Life

1908

Born on 12 October in Sevastopol. His father was Abram-Itskhak Moiseevich Korets, a watchmaker from the shtetl of Talne in the Uman governorate. His mother was Slava (Simkha) Filippovna, née Eisurovich.

He spent his childhood in Sevastopol and in Simferopol.

1924

In 1924 he left for Moscow. He worked as a bookbinder, a confectioner, and a loader.

From 1927 he studied at the Moscow Industrial-Pedagogical Tekhnikum1). The graduates’ page of MPSU calls these years, 1927–1929, study at the Moscow Industrial-Pedagogical Institute named after Liebknecht2). In 1929 he transferred to the physics and mechanics faculty of the Leningrad Polytechnic Institute and stayed there until 1932*. He heard lectures by Lev Landau and Matvei Bronstein.

1932

Sverdlovsk. He wrote to the investigator: “a research fellow at the Institute of Physics”**.

1935

On 13 March, at Landau’s invitation, he came to Kharkov as an engineer of the theoretical group of the Ukrainian Physico-Technical Institute3). At the same time he was Landau’s assistant at Kharkov University. On 31 December Landau wrote to Balitsky, head of the NKVD of Ukraine, that Korets had been his closest collaborator4).

On 14 November he was dismissed “for concealing his social origin”*. On 28 November he was arrested* on a charge of agitation against defense orders. On 26 February 1936 the court gave him a year and a half5). On 7 April the sentence was quashed. The ruling of 13 May says who sent the case back: the special collegium of the Supreme Court of the Ukrainian SSR proposed that it be heard again. The Kharkov regional court then sent it back for further investigation and left a written undertaking not to leave6). On 25 July the head of the 4th section of the SPO closed case no. 7771: there was no material for a charge7).

1937

Following Landau, he moved to Moscow. The physics department of the MSPI named after Bubnov, now MPSU*, and the journal Tekhnika Molodyozhi.

1938

In April, together with Landau, he wrote a leaflet8) against the Stalin regime and passed it to the student Kogan. In the court record the witness is named Pavel Davydovich9). In later testimony he calls him Lev Davidovich and says that he later took Kogan for an agent provocateur. There he also writes that in Lefortovo he was beaten and that the investigation’s testimony, apart from the admission about the leaflet, does not correspond to the facts10). The Wikipedia article, following Gorelik’s book, calls this Kogan the poet Pavel Davydovich11).

On 27 April he was arrested together with Landau and Yuri Rumer. On 25 November 1939 a military tribunal gave him ten years under article 58, points 10 and 11. The court dropped the charge of espionage*.

He served his term in Pechorlag, near Mezhog13). In 1942 another ten years were added: at the beginning of 1941 he had said that Germany might attack the Soviet Union.

1952

On 18 March he was amnestied. Fourteen years had passed in the camp. Until 1958, exile in Inta, in the Komi ASSR. He worked as senior engineer of the power network at the Intaugol combine12).

In 1956 Landau wrote to the military prosecutor’s office: Korets “had shown himself to be a capable young theoretical physicist”14). Rehabilitation in the 1938 case was refused. It was granted only after his death, in 199015). In the 1942 case he was rehabilitated in 1958.

1958

He returned to Moscow. He worked at the journal Priroda and wrote books under the pseudonym Karev. In Kvant, 1972, no. 4, his article with Z. Ponizovsky appeared, “Why is the Moon not made of cast iron?”16). His friendship with Landau lasted until Landau’s death in 1968.

1973

In December he filed papers to leave for Israel together with the family of his daughter Natasha and her husband, the physicist Yuri Golfand. Permission was not given: his son-in-law’s work was classified. The family left in 1990. He did not live to see that day.

1984

He died on 19 February in Moscow, at the age of seventy-five. The yahrzeit is 16 Adar I 5744.
