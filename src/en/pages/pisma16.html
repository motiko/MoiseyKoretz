Letter sixteen

7 May 1947. Mezhog –Leningrad

My dear little daughter! So neither my December letter nor your answer to it was lost, and I grieved over them in vain. It is simply that you answered me with a small delay of three months. Here I have no right to sorrow, and more than once I have been in the wrong toward you... I doubt that you were quite so terribly frightened on receiving Grandfather’s report of my illness. Otherwise you would have made more haste to answer my letters and the postcard yourself. A great weakness dragged on in me for a long time. But now almost all the consequences of the illness have smoothed away; there remain only a small trace on the heart ( any blood infection, even a local one, leaves an indelible trace on the heart muscle) and considerable scars under the chin. This letter will reach you just before the examinations, so you will hardly be able to answer it. But I beg you very much, on the very day the examinations end, to let me know the results, even in the briefest letter. It grieves me very much that I cannot be near you, little daughter, at this serious time. Perhaps I might even have helped in something; in any case I would have worried together with you. In Anatole France the hero of Contemporary History, Bergeret, despises every sort of decoration and title, but deep in his soul he feels that it is far more impressive to despise them while having them than while not having them. That is just how I think about the medal.

Not to mention a gold one, even a silver medal would have made admission to the university easier. But in any case, neither a gold nor a silver medal is a measure of a person’s mind and abilities. In L. N. Tolstoy’s Resurrection there is a phrase about one man ( I quote from memory): Being dull by nature, he had the misfortune to finish the gymnasium with a gold medal, and grew so convinced of his abilities that he became exceedingly stupid. Such a result can also come of certain successes on the stage, if one does not take the matter seriously and does not work on oneself. I have read the play The Russian Question. Simonov is a gifted and many-sided man of letters, but his poems are better than his plays. Yes. Try as I may, I feel that I am beginning to fall behind you in knowledge of contemporary literature. Of the four works you listed I have read only two: Wasilewska’s When the Light Comes On and Fedin’s First Joys. Of the writer Panova I have not even heard, and Panferov’s The Struggle for Peace I do not yet know either. My opinion of Wanda is extremely low. She does not write; she knits. But among things of recent times, Bek’s tale Volokolamsk Highway is splendid. Have you read it? It stands, of course, above Simonov’s Days and Nights. But highest of all stands They Fought for Their Country, by M. Sholokhov! There is the literary genius of our age, whom one may confidently set in the same rank as the classics of Russian literature. I am glad that you love Mayakovsky. I love him too. And have you read E. Bagritsky? Do read him. If you have read all of Shakespeare and Ibsen, you have read a great deal. Of Ibsen, one of my favorite writers, I shall write you a separate letter. Contradictory judgments of literary works should in no way frighten you, a future scholar of literature. Never mind what some respectable critic may write. Trust yourself. I am still working as a laboratory assistant and an X-ray technician. I get through from 7 to 4. From 4 to 6 I sleep, and in the evening I enjoy the solitude., I write, I think and, when there are books, I read. From my young years I have been poisoned by the venom of writing verse, and for me this is a delight. Only my poems grow sadder and sadder, and so I do not send them to you. But recently, thinking of you, I wrote a poem that is not sad. Come now, literary critic, tear it apart

Hello

Again the rails,

Again the train,

Again the distant lands.

Hello, morning,

Hello, sun,

Hello, my youth!

Along the carriages the wind whistles.,

Tears the black smoke to shreds.

It is good in this world

To be,like the wind, young.

It is good,like the wind ,to race

Into the unknown distance,

With the ringing morning

Racing,

Running through the towns.

Past the garden,

Across the field,

Only behind, the dust swirls.

Hello ,joy,

Hello, freedom,

Hello,tomorrow,

Hello, life.

A big greeting from me to Mama. I have received her letter and in the next few days will write to her separately. I kiss you warmly, little daughter, and wish you success. Your papa.
