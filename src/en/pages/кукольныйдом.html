“A Doll’s House”

N.G.

Musical performance

How familiar this play is to me

In the reading of a single act:

Act three of “A Doll’s House”

And the first of my grief.

At times life is sharp,

Sharper than the finest play…

And old Oslo comes washing

Over present-day Leningrad.

Ah, Torvald Helmer! By a verdict

Forever destroying our life,

Nora said to me, too, with passion:

"I no longer love you."

Five words, a pitiless stiletto,

Plunged into the trembling breast.

And to this day, of that moment,

I cannot recall it without pain!

You, Helmer, fell to your knees,

A hand covering the screaming mouth.

You followed the slender , fragile shadow

And listened to the slamming of the gate.

And I wisecracked, with a caustic smile,

Over my own furious pain,

Called Nora in jest a "man-eater"

And read her stories aloud,

Only so there would be no reproach

Where a reproach cannot help…

Do you remember this evening, Nora?

Do you remember, Nora, this night?

And beside the old sofa

I wept the whole night before you,

Not over a tale by Maupassant,

But over my own sick fate.

But in the morning you left, proud,

Having said to Torvald and to me

That you would live alone by a fjord

On the Petrograd Side.

Come, friend Helmer! Our grief

Has bound us fast together.

Come, let us talk of Nora,

gone to the fjord beyond the Neva.

17 July 1946
