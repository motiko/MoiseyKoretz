Interrogation record of Pyatigorsky

Interrogation record

5 December 1935

Pyatigorsky Leonid Moiseevich, born 1909, Fundukleevka, Kiev Region, Kharkov, 16 Tchaikovsky Street, apt. 12, Jewish, an engineer at UFTI, son of a merchant, wife Panina Ekaterina Anatolyevna, sister Tsilya.

Question: Do you know Korets well, and since when?

Answer: I have known Moisey Abramovich Korets since the end of 1934, from working together with him. I was not on friendly terms with him, but I was on good terms of interaction with him equally, as with the other workers of the institute.

Question: What do you know of Korets's counterrevolutionary activity and of his connections?

Answer: I know that in our institute there existed an anti-Soviet group, which included Korets, Landau, Shubnikov, and foreign nationals who had arrived from Germany — Weissberg, Martin and Varvara Ruhemann.

All the persons named above were closely bound up with one another and carried on in the institute work to wreck the fulfillment of special assignments of defense importance, and outwardly they disguised all this as personal discontent with the leadership of the institute and with the directorate.

Question: What facts are known to you?

Answer: The facts of the wrecking of such assignments are not known to me personally, but facts of agitation for sabotage of the fulfillment of defense measures are known to me from individual persons in the group. For example, at the end of this year Weissberg met me on the road and suggested that I go with him to a café to talk — "I'll treat you"; I did not refuse, and went in.

Weissberg at once began to express to me his discontent with his position at work, saying that our institute was now in a very grave situation and was heading for collapse, because the leadership of the institute, and especially the Party members, were unfit for this work and were dragging the institute down, not up, and that the work of defense importance had been wheedled in Moscow by the former deputy director Gey, and therefore the only way out of the situation that had arisen - was to have in the institute worker Party members who would not interfere with the work.

In the same spirit Landau, too, held purposeful conversations with me, saying that the scientific workers who were Party members and who stood at the head of the institute wanted to wreck the work of the institute, and that to this end they had taken on assignments of defense importance, which lowered the general level of the institute. On this same matter Korets, too, said to me that now they had posted guards everywhere in the institute, and that this created at UFTI an abnormal scientific atmosphere in which it was hard to carry on anything scientific, and everything was subordinated to defense work, in which certain persons had an interest, so as to hold on somehow in the institute, and therefore if we do not eat up Director Davidovich, he will eat us up, and the institute will go to ruin.

It should be noted that all the above-mentioned participants in the anti-Soviet group systematically visited one another, holding, in the same spirit, conversations among the scientific workers of the institute aimed at wrecking the work of the institute, especially in the matter of fulfilling the defense assignments.

Lately I was on the best of terms with Korets.
