Homeward

S.R.

Musical performance

Hoops, ribbons, sticks, little balls…

In a clear-voiced, merry throng,

Tired from the day, the girls, the boys

Come home late in the evening.

Home meets them with its usual tenderness,

Love, quiet, and coziness await them,

An old nanny with wonderful tales,

Cats and mice await them.

After work the streets will fill

With a crowd hurrying, unchecked,

People scold, jostle, frown,-

People are in a great hurry to get home.

Mothers, wives, with tender palms

Will wipe the lines from strained faces,

Serene life will fill with dreams,

Strength will return by morning.

Perhaps, to me, all this is – only seeming,

Perhaps peace does not wait for them at home.

Only…When will this at last come true.

Lord! How I want to go home.

The distant childhood was joyless.

Little that was good will you recall with kindness.

Beside my own people I grew up lonely,-

My house was not a home to me.

Years without sun, in fog and damp,

I lived through with a dream that would not leave:

Soon I shall grow up, grow up, grow up,

With someone good I shall go home.

I grew up.

The bitterness of struggle and suffering

A disheveled world flung in my face,

Hard after the meeting –the pain of parting,

A changing of countries and of lodgings.

There were bad ones and there were good ones,

But never and nowhere , not in a single one,

I did NOT find one that resembled a home,

I did NOT come home.

And now a sullen old age draws near,-

With the former strength and the former longing,

All night long I think, in torment:

Ah! How good it would be to go home, if only for an hour.

Often, gnawed through by unceasing longing,

To look for something in the half-dark of night

I went. I wanted to ask a passerby:

" Perhaps You know where my home is?"

No, he does not know. It is NOT worth even asking.

They will say the old woman is losing her mind.

Where are you, my home, adorned by a dream?

Where ? … I do not know myself.

Like a motley border it drags on, wearisomely,

Summer after summer, winter after winter,

I am a lonely wanderer upon the earth,-

Soon, oh soon, shall I come home!

28 November 1947

“R.S.V.”18 December 1947
