Memoirs of exile

Memoirs of Natasha, Moisey's third daughter, about the exile

In this photograph my mama and papa are sitting in an embrace at some party Both in round glasses and both tender and cheerful. I think that this is our first year in Inta, that is – the year fifty-one, winter. They are visiting and have had a little to drink – both free and unstrained – the first year together, as a family – a dream they had lived to see after all – whether for long, no one knew, but at this moment all is well.

Mama and papa met in the camp, in the North Pechora ITL, where both were serving their terms – mama under article 58\ 8 – suspicion of espionage – she was arrested in 38, as a member of the COMINTERN, one of those who came to the Union to help build communism in one country taken separately...

When she was arrested, she was working at the Institute of Red Professors, in the department of world economy, in the post of referent. She came to Russia in the year 34 from Latvia, then still tsarist, from Riga. She, as an active underground communist, was sentenced to arrest by the police authorities, and they searched for her everywhere. Comrades from the underground organized her escape into the communist paradise, which was very hard to carry out, but she managed it, and, once in the USSR, she actively joined in the Great Construction. Until, together with thousands of other Comintern people, she was arrested on suspicion of espionage and, after a short stay in one of the Moscow prisons – at the Lubyanka – sent to a camp, where everyone was given a splendid chance, working at timber-felling, and, croaking from hunger, to build Communism. Mama, ironizing at her own expense, loved to repeat the proverb – What we fought for, on that we got spiked...

They met very romantically. My papa was dying of blood poisoning he had an enormous boil on his neck – from undernourishment and vitamin deficiency,- and one of the comrades – the zeks lanced it with a kitchen knife, - without keeping to a single rule of hygiene \ no time to live high – if only to stay alive\. Naturally, after such an operation, it was hard to stay alive. And the next morning, unconscious, with a temperature over forty, he was taken to the local little hospital – to die... And my mama was working there then as an orderly. She brought him through by some miracle, and when he understood that he was still alive, he at once began to court her, and, although it was by no means simple\ by his own later account\ , he did after all win her over about a year later. And another nine months later\mama maintained that it was almost ten – she had carried past her time\ I was born. Mama was released in July of 46, when I was already half a year old. She went on working in the same place, so as not to lose papa, whose term was to end only after five years. This photograph was taken in 51..

Mama – Rudova Serafima Iosifovna. She was born in 1906 – in the city of Vitebsk, then her father moved with the family to Riga. When the Germans entered Riga, they burned all the Jewish hospitals, in one of which mama's mama was then lying, and all the rest of her relatives the Germans shot, or burned as well. That was the answer my mama got, when in the year forty-eight she went to Riga to look for the relatives still alive. It turned out, to judge by the documents given to her, that I am her only relative on this earth.

Papa – Korets Moisey Abramovich. He was born in 1908 in the city of Sevastopol, Later their family moved to Simferopol.

The second photograph was taken in the year fifty-six, in Inta, when Irochka came to us for the winter holidays. Papa in it is forty-eight, he had already worked for five years as senior engineer of power sales at the Inta-ugol combine, and we lived in two rooms of a two-story wooden house of the barrack type – one kitchen for the whole floor – and not a single toilet\ten families on each floor\. And still, after the dugout in which we lived for a year and a half, and which was flooded with icy water every spring,- this apartment seemed a palace. Next to our house stood a wooden watchtower, left from the time when the zone was still on this spot. But the children\not without my part in it\ once burned that tower. Despite the fact that by then papa was already counted a free hire, he was still obliged to check in once a month at the local militia station, as he was still under supervision, under the rules of deprivation of rights. And that meant that not only was he not permitted to live in another place, but he could leave for another city for no longer than a month, and that only after getting a certificate from the supervising organs.

In this photograph our papa is very joyful, because he is embracing two of his daughters at once. For complete happiness, by his own words, what he lacks at this moment is Galochka, his middle daughter, but at that time he had not yet found her. In the summer of this year we will go with him to Kislovodsk, and find her too.
