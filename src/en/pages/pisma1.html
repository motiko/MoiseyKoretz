Letter one

The year 1942. The war shakes the enormous body of Soviet Russia.Everything and everyone is on the move. In the north, near Arkhangelsk, a victim of the Stalin arrests, the 33-year-old physicist Moisey Korets, languishes in confinement.And in the center of the country, in the city of Ivanovo, are his wife and his 13-year-old daughter, who had fled besieged Leningrad . And now a letter arrives:

...After long wanderings your letter found me. During this time I have been through a great deal,but I have remained alive all the same, though no longer with my former health ( the letter bears a military censorship stamp, so that it is impossible to tell of the tortures during the interrogations). Your fate worried me very much, and I am terribly glad that you turned up with relatives in Ivanovo...I do not want to write about myself. Things are hard for me now. Parcels , it goes without saying,I do not receive.If you manage, then send a little parcel with something to smoke. Makhorka is now my only comfort , but it comes my way very rarely...

Irusya! I am writing you only a few words. For four years I have not seen you, and now I cannot even imagine what you are like. I do not know whether I shall be alive as long as is needed in order to see you. So even a letter from you will be, for me, part of making the acquaintance of my grown-up daughter.

I kiss you warmly.

Papa.
