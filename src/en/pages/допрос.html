Interrogation record, 12/XI-56

RECORD OF THE INTERROGATION OF A WITNESS 12 November 1956.

Military prosecutor of the department, Major of Justice Lebedev.

Korets Moisey Abramovich

b. 1908, city of Sevastopol.

Sr. engineer of the power network of the "Intaugol" combine

Married.

Jewish.

Higher education

Convicted in 1939 under Art. 58-10 and in 1942 under Art. 58-10 part 1 of the Criminal Code of the RSFSR Non-party City of Inta, Kirova, 30a, apt. 15 The mass repressions of Soviet people which were carried out in our country in 1937 and which I considered unnecessary, inexpedient for the cause of socialism, aroused in me a feeling of pain for the fate of the revolution in our country, a feeling of protest against these repressions. I had always., from my early years, been devoted to the cause of socialism and, to the measure of my strength and my possibilities, had fought for socialism. And when I saw that very many Soviet people were being arrested, and not only people who held high posts, but ordinary people as well, I came to the conclusion that such a policy undermines the cause of the revolution, the cause of socialism in our country, leaves us defenseless. Concrete facts of these mass repressions were very numerous in Moscow at that time; people spoke of it everywhere, and I saw it myself, although none of my relatives then was. Here I want to emphasize that I did not go into the rightness, into the justification, of these repressions, since I could not know for certain whether this or that person had been arrested rightly, with grounds, but I considered that such mass repressions were called forth by nothing, they were not needed; more than that, as I considered, they were harmful to our country.

And so, as a result of what I was living through, as a result of my conviction of the needlessness and harmfulness of the mass repressions, I wrote in 1937 that ill-fated leaflet for which I was tried in 1939. The writing of this leaflet is connected with a certain Kogan Lev Davidovich, 1918- 1919( I do not remember exactly ). I first met this Kogan back in 1925, .when he was 7 and I was -17. I saw him then at an acquaintance of mine- Margolis Lyubov Samoilovna. At that time I was somewhat engaged in literary work. Kogan, while still a boy apparently showed some leaning toward literature, and that is why he used to visit Marogolis, who loved gifted children and in some way helped them along. Of course, at that time I had no relations of any kind with Kogan, since he was still a boy.

The second time I met him was already in 1936, when he was a student at the Moscow Literary-Philosophical Institute, in his first year, and was himself beginning to write something. We met again at Margolis's, who lived on 3rd Tverskoy Yamskoy Lane. Kogan also came to my apartment some 2-3 times. He himself lived with his mother, but where- I do not know; I was never once at his place. Meeting with Kogan, we talked of literary subjects, and also of current events in the country and in particular of the mass repressions that were being carried out. Concerning these repressions I told Kogan my thoughts, of which I testified above. He agreed with me. And then once, in a conversation with Kogan which took place in March-April 1937 at my apartment, he told me that at their institute there was a group of students who wanted to come out against the policy of mass repressions, using terror for this, or something of that sort – I do not remember exactly now. To which I said to Kogan that to act by the method of terror was senseless, for it contradicts Marxism. I told Kogan that one must address an appeal to the masses, to the working people of our country, so that they would come out against the policy of mass repressions. Kogan agreed with me, but said that they, the students, would not be able to do this by reason of their small experience of life, and asked me to write a leaflet. I agreed to do this. At the same time Kogan told me that the leaflet had to be signed in the name of an anti-fascist workers' party and that he had a hectograph, and that they would duplicate this leaflet and distribute it among the population of Moscow on the May Day holiday.

Some 2 days after this conversation I wrote the leaflet and handed it to Kogan. I wrote the leaflet alone, consulted no one, and no one helped me. It seems that the next day after that Kogan came to me once more and asked for clean paper for printing the leaflet; I gave it to him, and he left me. That same day I was arrested.

Besides the leaflet I was charged with wrecking, with espionage, and with something else besides. The investigation was conducted with a gross breach of socialist legality. I was held in Lefortovo prison and subjected to beatings, and therefore all my testimony in the investigation, including what was in my own hand, does not correspond to reality, except the testimony that I wrote the leaflet.

My testimony in court likewise does not fully correspond to reality. I knew that if in court I fully renounced my testimony given in the investigation, my case would be sent back for further investigation, and I did not want that., therefore I testified in court to certain things that did not correspond to reality, but what exactly - I do not remember.

It must be said that already in court I guessed that Kogan had been a provocateur. Even during the investigation I gave testimony against Kogan, but the investigator for some reason did not enter this testimony in the interrogation record. In court I asked that Kogan be summoned to court as a witness, but I was refused this. After my arrest I did not see Kogan and do not know where he is or what has become of him. True, some 1.5 years after my arrest, after the trial, I had a visit with my wife, who told me that she had seen Kogan on a street in Moscow. Where Kogan is now, I do not know. My wife has died. About Margolis I likewise do not know, it seems in Tashkent.

It was on the basis of these facts about Kogan, who turned out to be at liberty, that I came to the conclusion that he is a provocateur. True, at the time when he and I were meeting I did not think this; I believed Kogan that there was a group of students who wanted to fight against the policy of mass repressions, and I reckoned myself part of this group, although of its membership I knew only Kogan.

The aim of writing the leaflet and of circulating it – was to address the masses with a call to rise in struggle against those who were carrying out mass repressions in the country, first of all against Stalin, whom I held to be the one to blame for the policy of mass repressions, and against those who were carrying these repressions out, that is, against the NKVD, its leaders. It is in precisely this sense that "the Stalin clique" is spoken of. I never thought that the policy of mass repressions was the policy of our whole party, of our whole government, and therefore I did not want the people to come out against the party and the government in general. At the present time, in the light of the decision of the 20th Congress of the CPSU, I can say with a clear conscience that in writing this leaflet I committed no crime. My mistake consists in this, that then, in 1937, I did not foresee that the party together with the people would in the end be able to overcome temporary mistakes. I thought then that these forces did not exist; I was in despair and wrote the leaflet, although I realize now that there had been no need to write it. But even having written it, I committed no crime, since under the Constitution of the USSR I had the right to freedom of speech, which is set in defense of the cause of socialism. And with my leaflet, I repeat, I wanted only to help that cause and did not contemplate anything against Soviet power, against the party.

Question: How are the words in the leaflet to be understood :"The economy is falling apart, famine is approaching"?

Answer: These words are to be understood in the sense that mass depressions may bring the country to the point where the economy falls apart and famine sets in, since there will be no one to work.

Question: How are the words to be understood: " Socialism has remained only on the pages of newspapers that have lied themselves out completely"?

Answer: This is of course too sharply put and untrue and an untrue formulation. What I had in mind was that the newspapers wrote about enemies of the people who in fact did not exist, and that the mass repressions hindered the movement toward socialism.

Question: What occasioned the writing of the leaflet: the mass repressions alone, or something else as well?

Answer: The writing of the leaflet was occasioned only by this, to come out against the policy of mass repressions. True, then and dl that, i.e. before 1937, I condemned and criticized, among my fellow workers, such measures of the party government as the prohibition of abortions, the extension of criminal liability to adolescents of 12 -14 years, the introduction of a passport system in the country, but this was my personal opinion on inessential questions. This had no bearing on the writing of the leaflet.

Question: At the beginning of the court session you declared that you had belonged to no counterrevolutionary organization whatever, and then testified that you had been a participant in one together with Kogan. How is this contradiction to be explained?

Answer: In the investigation I was accused of supposedly being a participant in a c.–r. organization of physicists: Landau and others. Nothing of the kind had existed, and that is why I said this in court. But when I was accused of having belonged to a counterrevolutionary organization together with Kogan, I testified on this point that I had indeed reckoned myself part of the group of students together with Kogan, and in that sense belonged to it, this organization, but did not consider it counterrevolutionary. Besides that, apart from Kogan I knew no one and carried out no organizational measures Question: From the record of the judicial proceeding it is evident that with your leaflet you came out against the policy of the party and the government, against " the clique of the party tops" How is this to be understood?

Answer: I. did not so testify in court, and in the record these entries are incorrect.I repeat, I was against the mass repressions and against those who carried them out, of which I testified above.

Question: In court you testified that concerning the leaflet you consulted Landau. Is this correct?

Answer: I did not say that in court; concerning the leaflet I never spoke with Landau. In the record my answer is written down incorrectly.

Question: In court you acknowledged the correctness of Landau's testimony on l.p. 24-25 concerning the leaflet. Is this correct?

Answer: I knew of no testimony by Landau; at the trial it was not read out to me.

Question: In court, in one instance, you declared that you had written the leaflet on your own initiative, and in another you testified that all this – was the result of provocation on Kogan's part. How is this to be understood?

Answer: When I spoke of my own initiative, I had in mind an appeal to the people concerning the mass repressions, while it was Kogan who proposed to me directly that I write the leaflet. whom I consider a provocateur.

Question: What were you arrested for in 1935 ?

Answer: In 1935 I was arrested and charged under Art. 58-10 for having spoken, in a wall newspaper, in criticism of the director of the Kharkov institute, where I was then working. I was sentenced to 1.5 years. But soon the case was dropped, and I was released, since I was not guilty.
