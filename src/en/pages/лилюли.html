Liluli

Steam from the deeply ploughed earth,

In the sky a little flock of cranes,

Children tumble about in the dust,-

Liluli has come flying,

Liluli – an illusion.

Hello, little Liluli,

Hello, little Liluli,

Hello, little Liluli,

Liluli – an illusion!

On the marsh by the river

The sandpipers began to whistle,

The brooklets began to murmur

With sleepless songs.

The old, dirty, gloomy forest

Seemed to have died and risen again

As green groves.

Both by the house and far away,

No sooner does Liluli draw near,

Lights are kindled

In golden arcs.

She raised a little white finger,

Took my glasses clean off,

And lay down upon my chest

With firm little breasts.

As if heartache had vanished,-

I did not start looking for my glasses,

A handsome fine fellow!

I am only twenty again,

I am a poet again, as of old,

Ready to give the whole world

To every passer-by.

And then the nonsense began!

Even a spiteful mossy stump

Exclaimed so: "Good day!",

That the mushrooms were embarrassed.

And voices come rushing:

" Well, even another half-hour,

Stay here at least half an hour,

Liluli – an illusion."

2–3 May 1947
