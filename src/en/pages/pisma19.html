Letter nineteen

21.12.47, Mezhoga

My dear little daughter! It is already some ten days since I received your letter, but I delayed the reply, because I was getting felt boots. It was not so simple. The question was not only of saving up the money for them, but also of having one of the free hired workers give up his voucher to me and buy them. For I have none of the ordinary rights and possibilities. Now the next question is before me, no less difficult, of sending these felt boots. The nearest post office is 5 kilometers from us, beyond the river. And again I have neither the right, nor the possibility of getting there. So I must again ask someone, and the one who does me this favor likewise has no official right to do it, and therefore runs some risk, the more so since my surname is known, because many people use the services of the laboratory and the X-ray room, and you and I have the same surname. And the main thing is that after the currency reform I have not a kopek left, and there is no one to borrow from either. So the sending will have to wait until the first days of January, when I receive my monthly pay of 25 rubles... It grieves me very much that, because of this delay, the parcel will arrive not for your birthday., but two weeks later. Besides the felt boots there will also be a second-hand batiste blouse in it. This is all I am able to send you for the day you turn nineteen, on which I congratulate you with all my heart. Now I turn to your letter.

It is natural that at the university, in the philological faculty, most of the students love Mayakovsky, since the people gathered here are, in literary culture, above the average. In my letter I wrote about your argument with your girlfriends about him, back in school. By the way, why do you assign the poem I Love to Mayakovsky’s early works? If my memory does not fail me, this is one of the last, if not the very last, of his long poems. I request a note, comrade literary scholar. It seems to me that this poem was written no more than 5 or 8 months before his death. The work in the drama circle, it seems to me, ought to be continued. In a year or two it will be clearer what this will turn into and which further path one ought to choose. Your strong points: a voice and musicality, may also be needed, and the first two years in the philology faculty will already give a general development. As for the torments of creation, they are necessary. Without discontent with oneself there can be neither movement forward nor achievements. By the way, the trouble now is that contemporary plays, even those that are not war plays, are for the most part male. Just yesterday I listened on the radio to a montage of Simonov’s play The Russian Question. Of the 10 characters only two are women, and even so their role is far from a leading one. The same in other plays. Even the performers, contrary to the old custom, are named all the men first, and then the women. However, it is possible that I am mistaken. How did the role of Maya Pelivanova turn out for you? She is, after all, much closer to you than Raisa. What are you working on now? How did the first semester examinations go? What did you take up in the winter vacation? this is not yet imaginative literature. Literaturnaya Gazeta»» Imaginative literature I manage to get, 2 or 3 books a month, and even then mostly old, read many times. The radio helps a little. But I can listen to it only a little, since in the daytime it is silent here, and in the evening I try to write and then, naturally, I switch off the loudspeaker. I am sorry that my poems did not please you. I purposely chose the most spirited ones, close to the tendency that is accepted now. Today I am not in the mood, but in the following letters I will send something more, since, from the stubbornness characteristic of me, I still want to send you something that would please you.

...There is still much of this sort in your life that I cannot picture, and so I cannot even put a question... Student life and student friendship are a wonderful thing. Reading these lines of yours, I was carried 18 years back, to when you had only just been born, and I was a second-year student. It was hard for me to study and to work, and still it was good. I would be glad if you were not too lazy to write more about your fellows... About my own life I too cannot complain, and I do not feel myself unhappy, although a man in my position, by the general rule, is supposed to feel unhappy and wronged by fate. I am not even going hungry now, since I gathered a decent crop of potatoes from a little garden plot which I laid out beside my laboratory, and until January I will have enough of it so that, adding it to the daily ration, I am full. And mentally I live a life no less intense than it always was with me. And though I envy the knowledge and the impressions which you are acquiring now, I would not exchange lives even with you... Tell Mama that Efim Minevich has already been here with me for several years, and he sends her his greetings. He is the elder brother of my friend from the same course, Solomon Minevich, also a student of the Karl Liebknecht Institute, where Mama and I studied in Moscow. Solomon is now a professor of mathematics in Stalino. A couple of times he sent me greetings through Efim, but I never got around to writing to him, whether from pride or from self-love. I cannot bear even the thought that any of my former comrades might pity me or think me a failure. Better that they forget me... To see you and to talk with you, little daughter, I want very much. I had hoped that this past summer I would see you. Now this hope will have to be put off until the coming summer. I myself cannot come to Leningrad.
