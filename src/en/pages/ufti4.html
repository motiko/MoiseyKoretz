Minutes of the UFTI party committee

Minutes of an expanded meeting of the UFTI party committee of 28.11.35 together with members of the scientific workers’ section, the engineering and technical staff and the aktiv Present: members of the party committee, comrades Kalmykov, Kupriyanenko, Barilenko, Bogatov, Prikhotko, Zadneprovsky, Dubiner, Mamonov; the scientific workers’ section and the engineering and technical staff and the aktiv - 68 people.

Chairman Barilenko

Secretary Slonim

Agenda:

1. On the preparations for a conference of young scientists.

2. Information on Korets.

HEARD: the report of Comrade Garber.

Garber: Korets arrived at UFTI in November 1934. He was taken on as a theorist. He had been recommended to us for postgraduate study, and he worked until recently, when for deception he was dismissed from the institute, since he had been exposed as a rogue. The Party raised the question of putting its own party house in order. Thanks to the verification of documents and to the putting in order of the party and Komsomol household, we found out that Korets, when he was being admitted to the institute and to postgraduate study, concealed his social origin. Korets set down in his autobiography everything positive in the past, but that his mother is deprived of the right to vote as a house-owner, and that his father was a member of an artel which leased a steam engine, he did not set down. From the Komsomol organization he concealed even this, stating that his father was a kustar, and only half a year later, when the Komsomol organization raised the question of Korets, of his face, did he bring a certificate that for concealment he had twice been expelled from the Komsomol. The directorate of our institute did right to dismiss Korets from the institute, since by his actions Korets disorganized the work of the institute. Now he has been arrested by the NKVD. This proves once more that Korets’s conduct was not accidental and that his subversive activities evidently had a deeper basis. Unfortunately, in our institute some comrades until the very last consider that Korets was innocently dismissed. They must now reconsider their opinion, for this is a serious lesson in raising vigilance. It must be noted that a lack of vigilance on their part did take place. People and their work must be studied carefully. Such a situation must be created in which rogues like Korets would have no possibility of disrupting our work, directed entirely to the development of Soviet science, to the strengthening of the socialist Motherland.

DEBATE

Gott. The Komsomol organization of the institute dealt with Korets several times even before his social origin had been clarified, and in the public, Komsomol and scientific work a number of facts were observed which compelled the organization to discuss his conduct in detail. Korets very deftly, artistically, over a number of years systematically deceived the party, the Komsomol and the public organizations. If one traces Korets’s path, it is the path of an adventurer covering himself with a Komsomol card for his class-hostile ends. Up to the last moment he tried to mislead the institute community on the question of concealing his social origin. He said that his father was a kustar, and that his mother had by mistake been deprived of the right to vote for a month, and then she was restored. We have materials which show with all obviousness that his mother is deprived of the right to vote even to this day. Korets was not used to work, as all honest toilers are. By all sorts of dark machinations he very deftly directed other people to create squabbles, to discredit the party and Komsomol organizations and to build his own career. It is no accident that Korets, being a theorist, from 1932 several times out of purely self-seeking interest drops scientific work, going over to administrative work. A number of facts can be cited which characterize all the baseness, the adventurism of this man. Therefore the Komsomol, having gone through all his actions in detail, classed him as an alien person directing his activity toward the breakdown of normal work, they expelled him from the ranks of the Komsomol. The very fact of Korets’s arrest says that we still do not know everything about his dark activity, and that the decision of the Komsomol, the directorate and the party organization concerning him was the right one. This whole case calls us to still greater vigilance, to a better discernment and to the timely isolation of the class enemy.

Fedotov: The Komsomol organization was right to raise the question of expelling Korets from the Komsomol. Our weak vigilance in taking such people on to work at the institute must be noted. Vigilance must be raised, and perhaps there are still people among us who are harming our work. The timely work of the NKVD, directed to the removal of such elements as Korets, must be welcomed.

Komarov: The country our endured a hard period of class struggle. In examining the case of Korets, it must be stated that we overlooked a class enemy in the person of Korets. To the NKVD, probably, more is known than to us. That he is not simply a crook, but a political crook, who wormed his way into many institutes of Sverdlovsk, of Leningrad and of UFTI; in our institute, though with great delay, we still turned Korets out of the institute. The class struggle is not finished, it must be raised to the proper height.

Chernets: Korets tried with all his might to discredit the party organization, the public organizations and the directorate. At the funeral of Professor Shteinberg, our respected colleague, who did much for science, everyone grieved over his loss, and after the speech at the memorial meeting by a representative of the party organization Korets said to those around him: “And how they lie, without a twinge of conscience.” It is no accident that we had to drive Korets out of the ranks of the VLKSM. Korets’s arrest is no accident. Korets - this is a political rogue. He did UFTI a great deal of harm. The class vigilance of the NKVD must be welcomed. Korets’s arrest obliges us to raise our class vigilance to the utmost.

Pyatigorsky: At a meeting of the VLKSM Korets declared: “Yes, I did indeed conceal my autobiography. But I ask you, send me to other work, rank-and-file work, where I could be reinstated in the Komsomol.” By this he wanted, with a recommendation from the Komsomol, to worm his way into another institute. While working at the university, he likewise by deceit wormed his way into a docent’s post, while he was an assistant; the director found out, called Korets in, questioned him, and he declared that this was some mistake. But that does not matter. Korets appears as a dishonest Soviet citizen who tried, by deceit and lies, to worm his way in, doing all sorts of base things. The VLKSM did right to expel him from the ranks of the Komsomol.

Vigdorchik: When Korets appeared in the VLKSM they did not take him at his word, but began to test him in practical work. At a Komsomol meeting we asked, what is your role in the theoretical group. He answered that when he began to work at the institute, Professor Landau began to work more. At the exchange of Komsomol cards the forms were filled in from oral statements, and Korets concealed his origin, which gave him the possibility of worming his way into the organization. Korets was an adventurer who conducted his policy skillfully, himself took part nowhere, and carried on demoralizing work through others. I consider his expulsion from the Komsomol and his removal from work to have been right. The organs of the NKVD, having arrested Korets, did a good thing.

Gusak: We are used to speaking of the class struggle as somewhere not among us, but that it is quite close among us - this we do not notice. The Soviet power and the party do right to burn out the class enemy with a hot iron. The organs of the NKVD took up the question of Korets in good time and removed him as socially dangerous. As of today Korets was still concealing his origin, indicating that his mother had been deprived of the right to vote for a few days and that his father was a kustar. Such students are expelled. By deceit Korets wormed his way in everywhere and carried on his wrecking work. He also made his way into UFTI by deceit, and his work among us was directed toward disorganization. Owing to the work of Korets and of those like him, owing to our insufficient class vigilance, the work of the institute has now been brought almost to collapse. Korets’s arrest ought to serve us as a harsh lesson in our further work.

Slutskin: One must analyze deeply how it could happen that Korets was able to carry on such work as led to all sorts of cliques, to whispering. Meetings are rare among us now, there is no public opinion around the struggle for the plan. This does not mean that our public organizations work badly, that we ourselves do not understand that all sorts of incitement by such people as Korets went on among us, and that there was no unity of ours. It is now a scientific necessity to unite the collective and to put a decisive end to all sorts of cliques.

Davidovich: Korets’s person is clear enough, and unquestionably he is a rogue and an adventurer. The case of Kara must be noted, who was likewise removed from his work for concealing his social origin. The scientific supervisors agreed that such people cannot be kept at our institute. But, unfortunately, at Korets’s dismissal Landau did not grasp the essence of this question; he fell under Korets’s influence and gave up his work at the institute, holding that Korets’s dismissal had been wrong. Some of the staff must reconsider their opinion concerning Korets’s dismissal. Korets’s assertion, that his mother had by mistake been temporarily deprived of the right to vote and is now restored, is untrue. The documents confirm that his mother is deprived of the right to vote and that no one had restored her up to this time. Landau must take firm account of this. Gusak’s statement, that at the present time the institute has been brought to collapse, is untrue. A collapse of the institute we do not have; on the contrary, we have achievements in the work, but if we had not had this year a struggle directed against defense themes, in which Korets played an organizing role, the institute would have had considerably greater achievements.

Barilenko: Lately we have had a number of cases of dismissal for concealment of social origin and for anti-Soviet work, but the community has discussed these questions little. Kara’s example was not worked over in the community. The second case is Shampanier. Shampanier worked at the institute for several years. Those who came into contact with him during the English lessons more than once noticed cases of his slipping anti-Soviet views through in the course of his teaching. This was plain from the speeches at the party committee of a number of comrades, but we attached no importance to this, and only thanks to a letter from Melikator, who did not work at the institute but came across him by chance and wrote us a letter, which we considered at the party committee, about Shampanier’s anti-Soviet views, where the facts of the letter were partly confirmed. At the party committee, from the statements of a number of comrades, we found other facts of Shampanier’s slipping in anti-Soviet views. The conclusion from this is that we are not vigilant enough, and for a long time we allowed people such as Korets to remain in our collective, who carried on subversive work at the institute in the fulfillment of the thematic plan.

Gorsky: When I came back from leave, there was talk to the effect that scientific workers’ pay would be cut until they passed the theoretical minimum. These rumors came from Korets. I knew that Korets, being a Komsomol member, was saying this not on his own behalf but from the organization. This had a negative effect on the work of many comrades. For pay is an incentive to work. Now it has become clear where Korets’s work was directed.

Garber: The comrades’ speeches were healthy, mobilizing us to greater vigilance.

Zlunitsyn’s proposal is adopted unanimously, with an addition on the exclusion of Korets for wrecking.

Chairman of the meeting Barilenko

Secretary Slonim

Resolution

The expanded plenum of the UFTI party committee with the aktiv, having heard the informational report of Comrade Garber on the arrest of Korets, a former employee of UFTI dismissed for deceiving the institute on the question of social origin. In the examination of this question in the Komsomol organization of UFTI, Korets tried a second time to deceive it, declaring that his mother had recovered her electoral rights, which in fact had not happened, for which he was also expelled from the VLKSM. While working at UFTI, Korets tried, and in part not without success, by means of lies and intrigues to create in the institute an unhealthy atmosphere which held back the fulfillment of the institute’s program. Korets’s arrest convinced us still more that in the person of Korets we have a class enemy who worked at the institute with self-seeking aims; the facts, so far few, known to the broad community of the institute, speak for it that the tactics and the method which Korets used in order to create an unhealthy atmosphere in the institute are proper to the tactics of a class enemy who had penetrated our institute and carried on demoralizing work. The expanded plenum of the UFTI party committee notes with satisfaction the work of the organs of the NKVD — the keen eye of the dictatorship of the proletariat, promptly cutting short some whatever attempts of the class enemy to bring disorder into the ranks of the proletariat, which is victoriously completing the building of socialism. At the same time the plenum of the party committee approves the measures of the public organizations and of the directorate, of the institute, which adopted with regard to Korets measures exposing his class face, and call for universal class vigilance toward all and every attempt of the class enemy, who is waging a struggle against the proletariat that has won. For its part the aktiv of the UFTI staff undertakes, despite the attempts of the class enemy to disrupt the fulfillment of the institute’s program, to fulfill the program of the current year.

Chairman Barilenko

Secretary Slonim
