Letter eighteen

16.11.47. Mezhog, the Protoka medical settlement.

Well done, Irushka!

Having had no letters from you for so long, I had grown quite downcast; I thought you had not got into the University and therefore did not want to write to me. But when today, an hour ago, I learned from your letter that you had achieved your aim, and that it had not been so very easy (since of the four of you, you alone got in), I was glad and proud probably no less than you, although there was not a fingernail's worth of my merit in your victory. Of course I should have liked to help you at least a little, but it remains only to rejoice from afar and to console myself with the thought that certain of your gifts, by the natural laws of nature, you received from me, but to take pride in that would be absurd. So I simply take pride in you and rejoice for you. True, it is still too early to crow. Your faculty is a slippery one: one may come out of it either a great specialist or a person fit for nothing. At the time when I was entering a VUZ, we had no philological faculties; there was, at Moscow University, the FON, the faculty of social sciences, which we then mockingly called the Faculty of Those Awaiting Brides. Now, of course, the times are different, and your faculty is much more concrete than those of former days.

If you have a copy or a draft of your paper On the Poet and on Poetry, be sure to send it to me. I too would have chosen this subject, although the subject of the humanism of Russian literature is also attractive to me, especially in its comparison with French literature of the 19th century. I very much wanted to know how you picture your work after finishing the university. Only sincerely and without any modesty. The very maximum. Your journal of collective creation interested me. Chio iy are you writing in it. I am very glad that you love Mayakovsky and defend him, even while in the minority. In order to love Mayakovsky one must in general know very well how to read and understand verse. For most people both the rhythm and the force of Mayakovsky prove inaccessible, behind the complexity of the notation and the images. But Mayakovsky is many-sided, and one cannot love all of him equally. The more so as he also writes with unequal force. For example, How the godmother was at Wrangel's was much weaker than the corresponding pieces by Demyan Bedny. Which of V.M.'s long poems do you like best of all? The one I love best is the long poem whose title, as ill luck would have it, I have forgotten, but the introduction to which is called About This. What do your occupations in the dram. circle consist of? Only rehearsals, or is there something else as well? For an actress your faculty will give an excellent preparation, but here one also needs talent and a great deal of will. And the main thing. an unbounded love of this work (read Ostrovsky's Talents and Admirers). Do you have both, in your opinion? Answer only without modesty; I cannot bear that quality. And refer not to the opinion of others, but only to your own. Despite my isolated conditions, almost all the plays and books you listed are known to me. I read Izvestia and Literaturnaya Gazeta regularly; above my writing desk I have a radio which, though it speaks in a whisper, can be made out entirely if one puts an ear to it; sometimes the thick journals turn up too...As you see, I am still spending my strength and do not wish to sink to the bottom...As I have already written you several times, the chief interest of my life now is poetry.

Since the beginning of '46 I have been writing poems systematically, working on them, trying to approach them not as a dilettante but professionally. Since you are the only one of my correspondents who is engaged with literature and, besides that, my beloved little daughter, with whom I should like to be more closely bound, your opinion of my work, on which I place the most serious hopes, would be very precious to me. I once sent you one or two of my poems, but you did not let fall a sound about them in reply...
