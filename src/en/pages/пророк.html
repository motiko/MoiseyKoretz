The prophet

In an unknown desert I lay upon my sackcloth and wept in despair over what I had left undone.

And the lord appeared before me in my own image, but the seal of the glory of the world lay upon his brow, and I knew him and covered my head before the majesty of my lord.

So I lay, stretched face down at his feet, and time ran its course above my head. And there was no border between my spirit and the stones of the bare desert, And the hot wind above it, and the stunted bushes among the stones, And the overturned cup of the sky and the blazing sun.

And the lord spoke, and I did not know whether it was my voice, or the Voice of the stones of the bare desert, or the voice of the hot wind above Her, or the voice of the overturned cup of the sky, or the voice of the blazing sun.

" Rise, Man, and name yourself Namieil. I will give you strength like the strength of the lion of the desert; supple and mighty, like the body of a boa, the limbs of your body shall become. I will give you beauty like the beauty of an Arabian steed racing before a herd of wild mares crowded in a mad run. And your face shall be like the morning dawn over the mountains of Lebanon.

I will give you a mind that penetrates the heavenly firmament and the depths of the sea, and by your mind you shall be like me and shall create worlds in your own soul and in the souls of human beings.

And thoughts, the children of your mind, in their glittering garments, shall wander the world, striking and consoling.

I will give you a soul, boundless as the ocean, and all the sufferings of the world and of its people shall pour into your soul, as streams pour into a lake among the mountains.

Behold, I have marked you with my finger, and from now on you shall burn and not be consumed until the end of your days".

And again I wept before the face of my lord, for I felt in my soul both the grief of a blade of grass gnawed through by an ant, and of the ant crushed by the foot of a mother weeping for the death of her daughter, and the sufferings of a girl who died of love for the beloved who had left her, and of the youth who had left her, hungering to accomplish and not attaining. And of the poor man on his dunghill, And of the rich man on his sumptuous couch, and of the slave in the quarry, and of the ruler on the throne of kingdoms. And the ocean of my soul could not hold the sufferings of the world, and, crushed by them, I gnawed with my teeth the dust at the feet of the lord.

And god went down on his knees before me and took my blazing head into his large, gentle hands. And with his living lips he kissed my lips writhing from pain.
