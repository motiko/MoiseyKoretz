Epilogue

A ghost - Rabbi Zkharya,

Having broken off his smooth tale,

In his high chair

Swayed steadily, slowly.

And onto his gray beard,

Hurrying one after another

Between the deep wrinkles of his face,

Large tears fell.

Even three millennia

Did not soften the rabbi's grief,

Even the eternal cold of death

Did not lessen the pain of loss.

And, should it seem strange,

That, having embraced the ghost,

Together with my ancestor I wept,

Keening in the Jewish tongue.

I wept for Noemi too,

My great-great-great-great-aunt,

And for all the other Noemis,

Who perished so senselessly;

For the untimely loss

Of those dear, beloved of the heart,

Whose death leaves

A bleeding trace in the heart;

For my incorporeal children,

Born in the pangs of creation,

And killed in the cradle

By an indifferent hand;

For my living beloved,

Defenseless, like Noemi,

From whom I have not the strength

To turn aside the cruel blow.

Rabbi Zkharya wiped away the tears,

Blew his nose into a handkerchief

And, having pressed my hand firmly,

Set about telling his tale again.

"What then, now, descendant,

Can I still add for you

About the exodus of Israel

From distant Egypt?

We wandered in the desert, '

We fought with the tribes,

Winning our sustenance

By plunder and by keeping cattle.

N, having kept the promise.

That he gave us at Sinai,

Our lord in every battle

Always gave us the victory.

I remember, once a flying detachment

Under the command of Egoyoshua

(I was not in that detachment)

Fought a cruel battle.

The Jews were already winning,

But the sun was sinking low

And the enemy hoped,

Having seized the children and the women,

To slip away by night from the rout.

And so that the Jews might manage

To cut down all their opponents,

So that the rich booty

Would not bolt from under the nose,

Navin asked of God

And Jehovah obligingly

Carried out Navin's request."

I coughed in spite of myself

And said timidly: -

"Rabbi! Is this miracle not like

Another - the one with the mosquitoes".

"What are you saying, what are you saying! This was

A trustworthy report

From those who were in the detachment,

The front-line correspondents!"

"Ah! Then, of course, I believe!

To doubt the reports

Of front-line correspondents

Is very bad for the health.

But I think that later,

After three millennia,

It will be possible to live in the world

And not believe in miracles."

"All right, all right, freethinker !" -

Rabbi Zkharya smirked, -

"Us, I see, nothing will teach,

No scuffle of any kind...

And that is all, dear descendant,

I have done a Jew's duty,

Having told you, descendant,

On this quiet Erev Peysekh,

Our ancient Haggadah.

Time, in a furious torrent,

Flows on through the universe

And carries off, past all return,

Much joy and much grief..."

Perhaps my splendid ancestor

Added something more besides,

But suddenly, as if waking,

Right above his very ear

My loudspeaker wheezed,

Relaying the striking of the clock,

And with the twelfth stroke

The ghost vanished.
