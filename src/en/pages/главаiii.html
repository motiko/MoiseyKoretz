Chapter III

Two camel-marches away

From the brick factory

The caravan road

Came up to a great ford,

By this ford, across the sea,

When a raid had succeeded,

They bolted out of Egypt

Onto the Sinai Peninsula,

Loaded with plunder

And with herds they had driven off,

All the brigands of the desert

Not subject to Egypt.

In a huge noisy camp,

Divided into tribes,

They settled beside the sea,

Forty thousand emigrants.

(By the biblical statistics

There were seven hundred thousand of them, -

But here I enter a correction

For the Jewish temperament.

I cannot help recalling, by the way,

A shabby little shop

That was kept in our shtetl

By the grocer Khaim Purets.

And him, throughout the shtetl,

Without the slightest irony,

Both to his face and behind his back,

They called - "a millionaire".

"Oh, our Khaim the millionaire!

God grant you such an income!

In a month, in his "store",

He has a clear hundred rubles!")

Rabbi Zkharya ben Reb Zorakh,

Like Reb Natan ben-Ezra,

Was of the tribe of Yehuda,

And therefore on the road

Their families kept together.

But of course, at the halt,

Aunt Dvoira would vanish,

Leaving the whole household

And all the trouble over food

Upon Reb Zkharya's wife, Hava,

Who was still nursing at the breast

Little Abrasha,

And upon gentle Noemi.

Reb Natan and Rabbi Zkharya,

Having given the camels their feed,

Decided, before supper,

To look over the Jewish camp.

Amid the chaos of tents,

Camels, oxen, rams,

Little of interest

The friends managed to see.

Suddenly Rabbi Zkharya noticed

That, scurrying among the campfires,

Barefoot boys

Were all running off somewhere into the field.

Hurrying after them,

Beyond the tents, in the field,

The pilgrims saw

Some five hundred Jews,

Young, cheerful, sturdy,

Drilling awkwardly

Under the command of Yegoishua

In the falling-in with arms.

One was puffing over an old bow

With a half-rotted string,

One was learning to throw a javelin,

One was waving a spear to no purpose,

One was hewing with a huge sword

(For the most part a wooden one,

Since of whole weapons

In the old weapons shop,

Which the Jews had looted

On the day they left Egypt,

There turned out to be very little,

And there was not enough for many.)

Rabbi Yegoishua himself,

Showing off before the troops

On a splendid pacer

Of an Anglicized breed,

Was in a general's uniform.

Rabbi Zkharya had once heard

From a tailor he knew

That this splendid uniform

Had been ordered three years

Before the events described,

But had kept wonderfully,

Since by Navin's wife it had been

Sprinkled with naphthalene.

The gold braids

Had not spoiled in the least,

And in all the boys

They set off a burst of delight.

"How do you like it, ben Zorakh,"-

- Natan nudged his neighbor -

"In Israel there have appeared

Generals and soldiers!"

"Well then" - answered Rabbi Zkharya,

No matter that swords

We have not managed to obtain: -

At a general's uniform

The pursuit will take fright!"

"Pkh! What pursuit is that!

Today, from the signals,

I read off on the telegraph

The whole makeup of this pursuit:

Twenty Libyan horsemen,

Fifty bowmen,

And four small

War chariots.

And when they see

General Jesus

And the whole host of Israel -

They will turn the shafts back around:"

The courage of Dvoira's husband

And his being so well informed

Astonished those around,

And one of them, with a bow,

Asked of Reb Natan

That he, if he could,

Explain to them the further route

And Moisey's intentions.

On hearing this request,

Rabbi Zkharya shuddered

At the looming prospect

Of eating a wholly cold supper.

But, alas! - Natan ben Ezra

Had already taken the bit in his teeth

And left without attention

All of Rabbi Zkharya's hints.

"Rabosai! With the guard

That Yegoishua has drawn up,

There is no sense in our leaving Egypt

In secret by the ford.

By the caravan road,

Known to all, and convenient,

We shall pass calmly, boldly,

Onto the Isthmus of Suez.

There work is going on now

On the digging of a canal,

And on it, as you know,

There are quite a few Jews as well.

Taking our brothers with us,

We shall set off to the north

And go along the coast

Washed by the sea

That is called the Mediterranean.

Three or four marches

From the Suez Canal

Will bring the two of us, brothers,

To that promised land

Which our god, from the sea floor,

According to Moisey's project,

Raised up, so that the Jews

might settle in it freely.

So that by the coming Sabbath,

Not a single day later,

We shall pitch our tents

In our new state".

And, snatching a scrap of leather,

Dvoira's husband, with a firm hand,

At once drew a map

Of the State of Israel.

(They say that this map

Came in handy for Bernadotte,

So as to tangle every question

Of the borders of Israel.)

Here there rose ahs and ohs -

Everyone, crowding, looked at the map,

Understanding exactly as much

As a ram in a pharmacy window.

Taking advantage of this moment,

Rabbi Zkharya tried

To lead Natan away with him,

So as to eat at least a warm supper.

But this time, too, the attempt

Remained unsuccessful,

And, with a wave of his hand, Zkharya

Himself was lost in listening to Natan.

And Natan, almost in verse,

Gasping with rapture,

Began to depict for the Jews

All the beauties of Palestine.

(A transcript of this speech

They printed word for word,

The Zionist newspapers of the Nineteenth Century)

Late at night, stepping

In the dark on the feet of the sleeping,

Somehow brought Natan

To their cart, did Rabbi Zkharya.

The stars glimmered tenderly

On high above the Black Sea,

Somewhere jackals were crying,

And hyenas were giggling,

Lonely dogs

Barked dully, half-asleep -

And the huge camp dozed

Between the sea and the road.

On the wagon Dvoira was snoring,

Sweetly smacking her lips;

Nestled at Hava's breast,

Abrasha was blowing bubbles;

Her arms flung wide,

Lit by the moon,

And herself lovelier than the moon,

Noemi slept on the carpet;

\\\\////

Having quickly eaten a couple of spoonfuls

Of tasty onion pottage,

Reb Natan fell asleep at once,

As if he had sunk into water.

And only Rabbi Zkharya,

Amid the sleeping universe,

Felt, sharp to the point of pain,

With every nerve, every fiber,

With every flutter of the heart,

With every sigh, a kandym glance,

The beauty of the fathomless night,

The first night in freedom.

Strange though it is, by morning,

And without a radio broadcast, Natan's report

Had become known to everyone.

And the alarming news

Of the Egyptian pursuit,

Gathering a tall tale about it,

Stirred the Jews up.

And while Moisey, in the tent,

Was conferring with Jesus, -

All the Jews, with no order,

Hurried to pack for the road.

Suddenly, in the far west,

A dozen kilometers off,

There, where the beaten road

Had crossed over a hill.

There appeared, flashing,

The copper helmets of the pursuit.

And down from the hill began to come

The war chariots.

"Egyptians! Egyptians!"

Yelled Aunt Dvoira,

And, lashing the camel,

Drove it at a gallop to the ford.

Everything that happened next

Was just as incomprehensible

As the battle pictures

In modern cinema-films.

Well, and since these films

You have already watched a hundred times,

I will not spend the time

Setting it before you in pictures,

That unutterable chaos

With which the whole Jewish camp

Rushed in a mass along the ford.

And in vain Yegoishua,

Who had managed to leap onto his horse,

Tried with his whip to bring

At least some kind of order.

The voice of the dread Navin,

Like a squeak, was lost in the noise,

And the general's orders

( Undoubtedly, of genius)

The army could not hear.

However, I must confess to you

That at that moment these orders

There was no one even to hear,

And still less - to carry out,

Since Yegoishua's army,

Having crushed Rabbi Moyshe's tent

And the owner of the tent,

In the rush, having rumpled him soundly,

Having put swords and spears to use,

Beat a path for itself to the ford

And, after Aunt Dvoira,

Ran across the sea.

Still, the warriors of Nalila,

Let us give them their due,

In this difficult moment

Did not abandon Moisey.

Taking the rabbi under the arms,

The young recruits

Managed to seat him

On the cart beside Aunt Dvoira.

Seeing that things were going badly,

And that the children of Israel,

Drowning one another, shoving,

Were pressing along the ford like sheep,

Jesus spat in anger,

And, cutting his horse with the whip,

Set off swimming around the crowd,

So as to overtake the fearless warriors.

Where are our heroes now?

As the reader knows,

Dvoira, Hava, and Abrasha

Had galloped off on the camel.

And Natan, Noemi, Zkharya,

Gripping one another hard by the hands,

Amid the frenzied crowd,

Barely kept on their feet.

Gradually lagging,

By Rabbi Zkharya's habit,

This threesome turned out

The last of all the last.

And they had barely managed,

Having left the African shore,

To walk a hundred meters by the ford,

Having stepped into the water knee-deep, -

When at once, right behind them,

Thundering like tanks,

Plopped heavily onto the water

The war chariots.

Harnessed with a team of six

Elephant-like horses,

And covered with armor, the chariots

Dread military machinery.

And already it seemed to Zkharya

That he felt, with the back of his head,

The blows of the horses' muzzles

And the hot breath.

One more instant -

And the heavy hooves,

Like little blades of grass, will trample

Rabbi Zkharya and Noemi.

And, reaping a harvest of death,

Like a relentless whirlwind,

The chariots will sweep

Along the column of emigrants.

And, drawing his neck into his shoulders,

Involuntarily quickening his step,

The rabbi, every second,

Waited for the fatal blow.

But some fifteen minutes passed,

And just the same Rabbi Zkharya

Heard, somewhere behind his back,

Neighing, stamping, cries, splashes,

And the clank of metal.

Seeing that he had not died,

The astonished Rabbi Zkharya,

So as to learn the reason of it,

Looked round with curiosity.

Here is what he saw then:

All four chariots,

Having barely left the firm shore,

Were stuck in the silt up to the hubs.

The young commander,

Trying to shift them from the spot,

Was laying on, in frantic, ripe oaths,

At Ra, at Osiris, at Isis.

And also at a hundred and forty barrels

Of pickled, of salted,

Fresh, fried, smoked,

And dried little godlings.

(Displaying in this business,

As Rabbi Zkharya noted,

Theological knowledge,

And, what is more, an undoubted

Poetic talent.)

As for the Libyan horsemen, -

They, while the commander

Was busy with theology,

Were not wasting time either,

But, scattering over the ground,

Picked up all the plunder

That was left from those who had gone.

Late in the evening the Jews,

Having suffered through a good deal,

Crossed over safely

Onto the Sinai Peninsula,

But the unfortunate Rabbi Zkharya,

Having almost come out onto the shore,

Managed to fall through

Into a pit full of water.

Fortunately, Hava and Deborah,

Having got hold of some brushwood somewhere,

Had already managed, by the time they arrived,

To get a huge bonfire going,

By which there were drying

Two dozen soaked through,

Chilled in the water to the bone,

Jews smeared all over.

Aunt Dvoira, the heroine

Of all of today's events,

Sitting proudly

On a broken little barrel,

For the hundredth time that evening,

In a hundredth new version,

Was hurrying to tell her husband

Of her experiences:

"When I, seeing the army,

Cried out - "Egyptians", -

Our great Rabbi Moyshe

At once heard my voice,

And at once, by some miracle,

Having jumped onto my cart,

Ordered me to drive the camel

With no road, straight into the sea.

And before my camel

The sea, by a miracle, parted,

And after me the Jews passed

Through the sea, as on dry land!"

"Just so, exactly, - as on dry land."

Grumbled Zkharya through his teeth,

Wringing from his clothes

Streams of turbid liquid.

For some four days Rabbi Zkharya,

Having caught cold that evening,

Wrapped his neck in a warm scarf

And sneezed every minute.

Rabbi Zkharya regularly

Answered her: - "as on dry land".

And Noemi, each time,

Clutching her father's shoulder,

So as not to stumble by chance,

Laughed till she dropped.
