Films and broadcasts

Veritasium. “I finally saw quantum mechanics with my own eyes…”, 4 November 2026. A video about superfluid helium. From 43:46: Landau is called a genius theorist. The video says there was some truth to the charge: citing KGB files, it says he was accused of heading a counter-revolutionary organization. From 44:13: the arrest on 28 April 1938, the Lubyanka, and Kapitsa’s letter to Stalin. Korets is not named.

“Landau — Three Conditions of Happiness. The Accident That Split a Genius’s Life in Two”, channel “Historical Profile”, 12 October 2026. This is a video essay, not a television film. From 29:56: his Kharkov acquaintance, the physicist Moisey Korets, writes a leaflet for 1 May. From 30:22: Korets himself, many years later, confirmed his authorship. From 31:06: Korets and the physicist Yuri Rumer are taken with him. Korets will spend almost 20 years in the camps and in exile. From 1:10:25: the author was most likely Korets, and Landau may have taken part in the editing.

Ilya Khrzhanovsky. The “Dau” project. It was planned as a film about Lev Landau, from the memoirs of his wife Kora; Khrzhanovsky began the screenplay with Vladimir Sorokin. Teodor Currentzis plays Dau, Yuri Alekseev plays the older Dau, Radmila Shchogolieva plays Nora, and Anatoly Vasiliev plays Krupitsa. Filming began in April 2007. The Institute was built in Kharkov in September 2009, on the site of an abandoned swimming pool, and was filmed there from October 2009 to November 2011. The Venice film uses footage from 2008–2011 and additional footage shot in 2024. On the project page, in the text about the 2020 launch, the film list opens with “Dau. Dau”. In January 2019 there was an installation in Paris, at the Théâtre de la Ville and the Châtelet. At the 2020 Berlinale the films shown were “Dau. Natasha” and “Dau. Degeneration” (“Vyrozhdenie”). The film “Dau” was shown in competition at Venice on 9 September 2026. The film’s site gives 3 hours 30 minutes including a 15-minute intermission; the English Wikipedia gives 195 minutes. Variety gives 202 minutes without the intermission. On 12 September Khrzhanovsky received the Silver Lion for direction. This is not the television film “Dau the Magnificent”. Reviews: The Hollywood Reporter, Jordan Mintzer, 9 September; The Guardian, Peter Bradshaw, 9 September; Screen Daily, Jonathan Romney, 9 September; Time Out, John Bleasdale, 7 September; Financial Times, Raphael Abraham, 10 September; Variety, Guy Lodge, 12 September; IndieWire, Siddhant Adlakha, 9 September; IONCINEMA, Nicholas Bell, 9 September; ICS Film, 13 September; El País, Elsa Fernández-Santos, 10 September; Desistfilm, Arnau Martín; Comingsoon.it, Mauro Donzelli, 9 September; Corriere della Sera, Antonella Catena, 10 September; «Медуза», Anton Dolin, 10 September; «Коммерсантъ», Andrei Plakhov, 9 September; «Новая газета Европа», Irina Karpova, 10 September; РБК Стиль, Stas Tyrkin, 10 September; Zima Magazine, Andrei Plakhov, 15 September. Dmitry Cheglakov. Mintzer’s review names the physicist Kortez, played by Dmitry Cheglakov, beside Shubnikov (Dmitri Tcherniakov): both are coerced to obey or face ruin. The review’s Kharkiv years are 1933–1937. It does not tell of Korets’s leaflet, the arrest on 28 April 1938, or the camps. The Variety cast list and the Desistfilm cast list name Cheglakov and do not discuss the part. The film’s site and the English Wikipedia cast list include him and do not name the part. Anton Dolin in Meduza writes that in the first part three scientist comrades of Dau are played by the opera director Dmitri Tcherniakov, the poet Demyan Kudryavtsev and the cellist Dmitry Cheglakov; the surname Korets is not used there. Plakhov in Kommersant and in Zima Magazine names Tcherniakov and Kudryavtsev and does not name Cheglakov. On IMDb the part is credited as Koretz, in Dau (2019) and Dau (2026). The 2019 entry, French premiere 24 January 2019, places Shubnikov (Tcherniakov) and Zlatogorov (Kudryavtsev) beside him. IMDb has no photographs. The Moscow Conservatory page names the cellist Dmitry Dmitrievich Cheglakov, concertmaster of the cello section of the State Symphony Orchestra “New Russia” since 2003. It says he appeared in Khrzhanovsky’s film project “Dau” as Landau’s comrade, the physicist-mathematician Korets, in the film “Dau. Dau”, and the biography still says the release is expected. The page has no still from the part. His channel is concerts and his own recordings, not the film. No clip was found that labels Cheglakov in the role of Korets: none by title, and none in the captions that could be read. The trailers do not name this part, and the frames were not compared with his portrait. The published casts of “Dau. Natasha” and “Dau. Degeneration” do not include Cheglakov. His IMDb page lists only these two “Dau” films.

Valeriya Korennaya. “Lev Landau: ‘All That Is Boring Is Very Harmful’. Part 2”, the program “Wings from the Attic”, Davidson Radio, broadcast of 7 November 2023. A radio program about Landau. From 11:36: the leaflet became the cause of the simultaneous arrest of three physicists, Rumer, Korets and Landau.

“Landau, Theorist and Man”, channel “NB KhGU”, 18 April 2018. A lecture about Landau. From 17:32: the speaker reads the story of the leaflet. The sentence is the same story: April 1938, the leaflet, the arrest of Landau, Korets and Rumer.

“The Smartest Jew in the USSR: A Genius Who Was More Dangerous Than a Weapon”, December 2025. A video about Landau. It is now closed, and the minute was not fixed. In the automatic captions, while the video was open: “Korets claims that you took part in composing this text” and “Korets has already given testimony.”

The Channel One film “My Husband Is a Genius”, 2008. Boris Gorobets. “The Film ‘My Husband Is a Genius’ and Waves in the Cultural Layer”, “Notes on Jewish History”, no. 12 (103), December 2008. The screenwriter, whom Gorobets calls Maria Radzinskaya, said: “And about Kharkov, about Korets, I had to take that out.”

“Lev Landau. More Than Love”, the Kultura channel. The automatic captions do not contain the surname. “Ten Commandments of Landau”. There are no subtitles, so the spoken words were not checked. “Dau the Magnificent”. There are no subtitles, so the spoken words were not checked.
