Letter twenty

18.9.48. Mezhoga

My dear little daughter!

On the 1st of August , in reply to your long letter, I wrote you an even longer one, and also enclosed a poem on some 4 sheets . Around the 17th of August two acquaintances of mine were to see you in Leningrad. I waited patiently, waited for a letter from you, but never received one. Then I decided that perhaps you were not in the city on those days. But on the 1st of September classes began, today is the 18th , and still there is no letter... Do not think, little daughter, that I take offense at you when you do not write to me for a long time: I understand that with that intense and filled life which you lead, you have no time for letters, but I very much want to know even a corner of this life of yours. ...My life goes on without any particular events. Materially I am not so badly off now: I have about 500kg. of potatoes, so for the coming months I am supplied with food. And the question of food is the most important thing here, and around me there is hardly one person in 50 who could say, as I can, that he is full. And with my being undemanding about food it is all the same to me what I fill up on, and I do not suffer from the lack of meat and various delicacies, so long as there are potatoes. And the question of clothing is settled more simply here than anywhere else. Requirements are lower, and if a man is all but in rags, he already counts as decently dressed. Worst of all, this year books and magazines have almost stopped turning up. If it were not for the radio and for the Literary Gazette.,which Papa sends me, I would be cut off from everything entirely. And now you too, my important thread of connection with the world, have stopped writing...I do not know whether I shall be able to get hold of anything this year for your birthday. The production of felt boots has closed down here, and now we produce nothing except Koch's bacilli( tuberculosis). But luckily I do not have them yet . I maintain that they are afraid of me,since I look at them "with an armed eye..." (Volga-Volga)
