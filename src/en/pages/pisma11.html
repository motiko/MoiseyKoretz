Letter eleven

16.12.45. Mezhog - Leningrad (Mother and I have returned from a four-year evacuation to our room, which by a miracle survived).

My dear little daughter! This letter will reach you between the New Year and your birthday. I do not know which it will be closer to, but I want to congratulate you on both. The New Year! I have already met so many of them here, sometimes with hope, sometimes with despair. I met the New Year of 1945 with the hope that the war would end and that I would be freed. The first came to pass, but it did not bring the second with it. And now I have no hope left at all. I do not know whether I do right to write to you again. All the same, we shall hardly ever see each other. Perhaps it would be better for you to forget me, and to build your life as if I were not in the world at all. Soon a mountain of questionnaires will rise before you, taking an interest in me among other things, and each time a hard problem will stand before you. (That your father is an enemy of the people, sitting in prison.) I find that the best thing in such a case will be to state that I left you in 1932, when you were 3, and that what became of me afterwards is unknown to you. You are a grown person, Irochka. At your age I was already living on my own. That is why I venture to write to you about this. That is why I see nothing wrong in it if you, though it will be hard for you, because I know that you love me, whether I deserve it or not, do not answer my letter. And I shall write to you once a year, for your birthday. I only ask you and your mother to let me know the main events of your life and any change of address, and to keep Grandfather Abram, whose address you know, informed of the same. Oh, my own one, it was hard to write all this, but I am glad that I wrote it all the same. You will turn seventeen.

This year you are finishing the ten-year school. You have one more year to study. In my opinion it is rather late to finish secondary school at 18, but since this is my fault and not yours, and since together with the country you lived painfully through 4 years of these 18, this minus too must be turned into a plus. Elderly parents always consider it their right to lecture their children who are just beginning to live. By my own life I have earned this right less than others, especially with regard to you. But I have thought a great deal about the experience of my life, and I want to pass on to you some practical conclusions, not in any order, but the first that come to mind. 1. You are finishing school quite grown up. If you want to save time, then for the time that remains turn your main attention to foreign languages. The other subjects the school system itself will pack into your head, and this, strange as it may seem at first glance, is the most important thing of all. If there is a chance, join a suitable circle or evening courses. Devote part of the summer holidays to this. In any case, see to it that by the time you finish school you have a free command of at least one foreign language. 2. Try to choose a specialty for yourself a year before you finish school. If you are thinking of entering a higher school, choose in advance which one, and which faculty, and pursue your choice stubbornly until it is fulfilled. Fortunately, Leningrad has the full range to choose from. If you choose music or singing, do not be upset if you do not get into the conservatory at once; then go first to a music tekhnikum. Do not think that singing, or work on the stage, is a less serious occupation than any other. And a cultivated singer's chances of earning are no lower than an engineer's, and there is more independence. These two pieces of advice are the most general. It is hard to write in more detail when I know nothing of your possibilities and cannot myself take any part in your life. Well, one thing more. Read more Russian classical literature, and trust it. Much that is good is stored up in it; life is truly reflected in it. Think, and try to do what is right.

You will say that what I wrote at the beginning of the letter is wrong, a compromise with conscience. It is not true. It is a forced departure from the truth, for which you are not to blame, but rather that which makes this departure necessary for you. Think it through, and then you will be able to do as I advise without leaving a stain on your conscience. If you find it possible to correspond with me, I shall write of much more. If not, then it only remains for me to write that wherever you may be, and whatever may happen to you and to me, I shall never stop loving my little daughter, whom I myself swaddled, whom I myself fed from a bottle, and in whose face and manners I always found so much that was like myself.

I kiss you warmly, my own.

Your papa Misha

P.S. And still, it is better if you write to me.
