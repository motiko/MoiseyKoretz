Letter six

4/11/44

Judging by your latest letters, you had all but made up your mind to move to Ivanovo, but in the end stayed on to study at the village school. I do not know the reasons for this choice, but the fact itself grieves me deeply. However hard it may be, one must always strive toward the higher level, especially at an age when a personality is being formed, when aspirations are vivid, when a girl begins her first romances. Of course I have a very poor idea of the present-day village, and the city of Ivanovo itself is far from a capital, but still the difference must be enormous. I think with horror of winter in the village, of that low level of intellectual demands and possibilities which reigns there. I am badly starved now, and thoughts of food and of being full are my chief thoughts, but if I were offered the choice of going on with a hungry existence in the city or the most well-fed life in the village, I would choose the city. The mere fact that day after day, month after month, you see only the very same people narrows, at your age, the possibility of improving yourself to such a degree that a delay, and even a degradation, of development is inevitable.

I do not know whether I have the right to give advice; I drew away from your life too long ago and too far, but if you and Mama accept my advice, then try at all costs to obtain your transfer to study in Ivanovo, counting it a half-measure, counting that only in Moscow or Leningrad will you be able to finish your secondary education.

It is very good that you did not manage to enter a technical college this year. Not because one absolutely must finish the ten-year school - of that I shall write later, but because a technical college in Ivanovo would have tied you to this very Ivanovo for many years. If one may even tozhno allow finishing school in the provinces. then in no case may one agree to study at a technical college or at a VUZ in the provinces.

As you know, all my life I have been taken up with VUZes and technical colleges and have come to the conclusion that neither the program, nor the teachers can create equality between an educational institution in the capital and a provincial one. For a technical college and a VUZ, traditions and level, the libraries and laboratories that have been built up, play the decisive part. Therefore it is better to enter a year or two later, but to enter only the very best educational institution in the given specialty, and without fail in Leningrad or Moscow.

Hence the answer too: so long as you remain living in the village of Gorbovo, you should in no case enter a technical college. If next autumn you move to Leningrad, the question will be decided in practice and depending on my fate. First, that immediate material reason which you stated in your letter. By entering a technical college, you would receive a specialty sooner. Besides, at a technical college you would find yourself among older and, possibly, more developed comrades.

As you see, little daughter, your point of view and mine have come together. Now it remains only to discuss which specialty's technical college one ought to choose. Since you and I agreed that a technical college gives you a specialty only just in case, and that only a VUZ will give the final specialty, the choice of technical college must be made from this point of view alone. Write me your thoughts on this and, in general, which specialty draws you.

For my part I undertake to give you a detailed and systematic survey of all the kinds of activity of people who have a higher education.

I am ending the letter. I beg you, Irochka, to hurry the sending of the next parcel. The makhorka you sent me will barely last through the holidays, and after that my torments will begin again.

Do not ponder the contents of the parcel; let there be samosad, but more of it and stronger. The packet kind turned out weaker than the samosad.

I kiss you both warmly.

PS If you were issued peas for your labor-days, send a little of that too. ( In the village Mama and I worked and were paid according to the number of labor-days, which were issued in kind, i.e.

in produce.- E.K.)
